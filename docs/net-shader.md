# The network shader (WP3, `?geom=net`)

`src/scene/vessels/net/shaders.ts`: one vertex and one fragment program for a whole network's mesh. Status: compiles and links (4 programs: base, VEIL, GLOW, VDBG), 9 varyings, 11 attributes, 9 samplers. The GLSL has never run: no device, no browser in the cloud. A CPU oracle (`tests/net-oracle.ts`) holds the formula by hand.

## What is shared with the old program (moved, not copied)
`glsl-shared.ts` holds the chunks both programs splice: `CIRC_WIDTH_OF_GLSL`, `STUMP_K_GLSL`, `CAP_CARVED_GLSL`, `PROCEDURE_MARKS_GLSL`, `FADE_ALPHA_GLSL`, `HIGHLIGHT_ALONG_GLSL`; `circuit/shader.ts` holds `CIRC_PEEL_GLSL`; `materials/shaders.ts` the fade functions. The old program's output is byte-identical to before (`tests/glsl-shared.test.ts`). Per-vessel names are global variables the generated `loadEdgeVS/FS` fill from the edge-state texture.

## The map-pose offset set (plan change 22 Nov), `tests/net-mapset.test.ts`
`NetMesh.offsetMap` (optional until WP2 bakes it; same layout as `offset`, a tube vertex's equals its `offset`) and three attributes `offM0..2` in the vertex stage. The stage keeps the two sums, `sum w F o lumen` (body set) and `sum w F oM lumen` (map set), and blends them by the vertex's bone-weighted peel share, so share 0 is the body shape and share 1 is the map shrink-wrap; with no set the result is unchanged. WP2 bakes the set with the offsets rebuilt by `oM_i = F_i^T (target - centre_i) / lumen_i` at the map pose (tests/net-mapset.test.ts does the same for a stand-in target). Normals still blend from the one bound set: a second normal set is not in the plan and is the next gap to measure once the real wrap exists.
Measured with a stand-in (each junction vertex projected onto the live map union, no smoothing): at the map pose the vertices land on the target; at t = .25 / .5 / .75 no flips and stretch <= 3x on Y, T, FOUR, CHAIN, H, STUB, TT. HS and HT (short merged middle edge) reach 3.1x at t = .5 and 5.1x at t = .75 (HT: one flip): todo, WP2's real wrap with its tangential smoothing is what must fix it. The varying budget is unchanged; attributes go 11 -> 14 (the 16 floor).

## Junction vertices: implicit skinning (WP3 ruling, 22 Nov), `tests/net-implicit.test.ts`
Rule (review doc): a junction vertex starts at the bone blend of its body and map offsets, projects onto the live smooth union of the region's capsules by three Newton steps, and takes that union's gradient (at the shade width) as its normal. Tubes stay on bones.
What was built: `net/regions.ts` (per region: up to 8 capsules of 8 bones each, mates, one texel row; the mesher now reports its capsules in `NodeReport.caps`), a `region` vertex attribute (-1 for tubes), `regionTex`, the SDF union and gradient in GLSL, three Newton steps, the shade normal with the ring ease, and the ink / sag / lift pushed along the sharp gradient. The oracle is `junctionPose` in `tests/net-oracle.ts`. Attributes 15 of 16; varyings 9; the program still compiles and links in glslang (4 variants).
Two things the numbers forced, both root causes of the earlier bone-blend failures: (1) a junction vertex's offsets must be scaled by the REST radii at the start, not the live ones: a far offset scaled by two different live radii folds the fillet, and the projection cannot undo a fold (the swell 1.2/.85 flips went from 19..43 to 0); (2) the ink must push along the sharp gradient, not the soft shade normal, which tilts up to 50 degrees from the surface at a crotch.
Measured on the nine fixtures: every junction vertex lands within 0.01 R of the live union; the normal is within 3 degrees (p99) of the finite-difference normal of the union at the shade width (where the ease to the tube's normal has not begun); no triangle faces into the union and stretch p99 is at most 3x (1.2 to 2.1x) at rest, the map pose, t = .25 / .5 / .75, zoom-out and swell 1.2/.85. Open, as todo tests with numbers: over the app's real swell range (engine D through widthSwell over the 20 presets: lowest 0.36, highest 1.17, +/-10% gives 0.32 to 1.29; worst arm ratio at one junction in one preset 3.17) folds of 1 to 6 triangles per fillet appear, not only for a differential swell but also for every arm at the low end (0.4) or the high end (1.27): the start at the REST radii is too far from a uniformly smaller or larger union for 3 Newton steps. Sweeping every low/high assignment over the arms (0.32/1.29, 0.4/1.27, 0.6/1.2, 0.5/1.0) the worst counts are 3 to 11 triangles of a few hundred (0.6/1.2: 0 to 1; 0.5/1.0: 1 to 3), by fixture: T 9, FOUR 8, H 7, STUB 10, HS 5, HT 3, TT 11, Y 0. A fold inside the real range is a root bug (the start arrangement, not convergence: 20 steps do not remove them); the fix is WP2's baked fitted starts. Stenosis waist, circBoost and least width are not in the swell numbers; they are held by the zoom-out and map states. HT does not converge in 3 steps (0.15 R) at a 4:1 ratio; presence .3 leaves 1 to 2 triangles of the shrunk arm facing in; the ink hull reaches 1.16 to 1.2 of its width at the sharpest crotch vertex on T, FOUR and TT at swell 1.2/.85. HT has one sliver facing in at rest (WP2's). The bone-blend-only numbers above (net-deform) are the projection's start, kept as a baseline.
Cost (22 Nov, second ruling): the capsules are evaluated ONCE per drawn frame by a tiny render-to-texture pass (`NET_CAP_VS` / `NET_CAP_FS`, `capPointIds`; one gl_POINT per region x capsule x point, written to `capTex`, RGBA32F, REGION_CAPS x REGION_PTS across, one row per region). It prelude-shares `netBone` (lite) with the vertex stage, so the live radius rule is the one GLSL function; there is no CPU twin. A junction vertex now reads `capTex` only: one plain texel per capsule point (capsules x 8) instead of a bone decode each (3 bone texels, plus the edge row, 27 texels wide, per bone). That is 8 x fewer reads in the dominant term but still up to 64 texels for 8 capsules (about 24 to 32 for a real 3 to 4 arm junction). The ruling's 16 or fewer is not reachable by sampling fewer points: measured at rest, 8 points per capsule keeps the projection within 0.05 R of the mesher's vertex, 6 points 0.07 R, 4 points 0.27 R, 2 points 0.56 R. Wiring the pass (the render target, drawing `capPointIds(regions)` as points once per frame before the scene) is WP4's; the program compiles and links in glslang (5 programs). Not measured on a device.

### Fitted-start experiment (plan owner's rule, 22 Nov): measured, NOT adopted
Rule tried: start = full + sum_i (1 - s_i)(absent_i - full), s_i = live / rest radius clamped to [0, 1]; for s > 1 the bone blend takes the live radius; then the 3 Newton steps. WP2's baked absent fits do not exist yet, so the oracle has a stand-in (`absentFits` in `tests/net-oracle.ts`, rest pose, `START.grow` / `START.fit` / `AXIS.on`, all OFF by default and used by no test): absent_i = each region vertex projected onto the union of the other capsules (30 steps), or, with `AXIS`, a vertex held by arm i's bones goes to arm i's axis. Swept per fixture: each arm alone at 0.32 / 0.5 / 0.8 / 1.15 / 1.29, all low/high assignments of 0.32/1.29, 0.5/1.0, 0.6/1.2, and every arm equal. Worst inward-facing triangles of a few hundred, by fixture Y / T / FOUR / H / STUB / HS / HT / TT: rest-radii start (today) 0 / 9 / 8 / 7 / 10 / 5 / 3 / 11; fit only 0 / 59 / 65 / 19 / 31 / 16 / 67 / 61; grow only 3 / 44 / 71 / 9 / 49 / 7 / 101 / 43; both 60 / 77 / 101 / 137 / 83 / 134 / 112 / 74; fit with axis collapse 85 / 50 / 293 / 241 / 128 / 236 / 185 / 47. Newton convergence is not the problem (worst residual 0.008 R at 3 steps, except where a vertex lands beside a collapsed arm). Why: the sum over arms overshoots when several arms are small together (4 arms at s = .32 give a total weight 2.7), and the live-radius blend for s > 1 is the start that folds the fillet (the swell 1.2/.85 flips of 19 to 43 before the rest start). With these stand-ins the rule is worse than the rest-radii start; it needs WP2's real fits to judge, or the weights normalised.

## Sewn ends (plan owner, 22 Nov), `net/sewn.ts`, `tests/net-sewn.test.ts`
Every arm that is not a permanent vessel (collateral, shunt, TIPS, DIPS, REX, feeder) is sewn into its host instead of being a fillet arm; every permanent arm is a fillet arm. Built (WP3's share): `sewnEnds(skel)` gives per edge and end the host's BONE (the widest permanent edge at that node) and the flags (inside, sink, flare, capped) from shunt-ends.ts' own rows (the rest: inside, flared, capped as `sewnEndAt` makes them); the edge-state column `sewn` (hostA, hostB, flagsA, flagsB; written once by WP4); `sewnCollar` in the vertex stage, 2D's `sideEndScale` and `flareScale` constants and rules, applied to the bone's swell so ink, stent cells and fade all follow; `sewnTex` (2 wide, one row per edge), written once per frame by `NET_SEWN_VS` (one point per edge end, the host's bone read by the same lite `netBone`, so the radius rule stays one function and the collar never reads a second edge's row in the middle of the first's); 6 glslang-clean programs. The oracle's twin is `sewnCollar` in `tests/net-oracle.ts` (2D's functions, `LiveState.sewn`). On the real skeleton 16 edges have sewn ends (C1a..C9, C8, TIPS, DIPS, S_PC, S_DSR, S_MC, REX; S_DSR's start is a run-on from the divided splenic vein, flags 0).
Node check (C3, C9, DIPS, both ends, at rest and at the host's lowest real swell): the end ring plus an ink of 0.15 of the host's lumen stands within the host's wall; the bones nearest the end are never wider than the arm or its host. Not done here, WP2's: the mesher still has to leave these arms out of the junction regions (so CONF keeps 3 arms), end them at the host, and bake the keys; wiring the sewn pass per drawn frame is WP4's.
Risks measured, not fixed: (1) the collar's narrowing reaches 0.6 host radii but bones near an end are far apart (C3 at PVH_L_1: first bone 0.026 from the end, 0.6 R = 0.012), so only the end bone carries the narrowing and the tube lofts from it to the next bone (more bones near a sewn end would fix it: WP0). (2) C3 at EPI_ILI is wider than its host at rest (arm 0.0116, host 0.0078): 2D also narrows a shunt toward its host's caliber (`endShare`, shunt-ends.ts, down to 0.4 of its own width), which is not in the collar and not yet in the net shader.

## The keyed start for wide junctions (plan owner, 22 Nov), `tests/net-keys.test.ts` (oracle: `bakeKeys`, `keyWeights`, `keyedPose`)
Measured first, the bake radius: geometric mean is worse than max / 1.2 (upward swell folds more than shrinking: run-on pair fold-free from 0.6 to 1.2, the other arm from 0.5 to 1.4; at 1.73 the pair folds 10 to 33). The keys: a direct projection of the rest arrangement onto the union at a far state folds (corners of [0.4, 1.15]^2: up to 5 triangles on FOUR, 2 to 3 on T, STUB, HT, TT), but a CONTINUATION bake does not: ramp the arms' radii from rest to the key in 24 steps, each step projecting the previous step's vertices by 3 Newton steps: 0 folds at every corner on every fixture with a run-on pair (T, FOUR, STUB, HT, TT). Runtime: the start is the convex blend of the keys' vertices, weights 1 / (log-radius distance + 0.001)^p, normalised, so it cannot overshoot; then the usual projection. 4 keys (the four corners), p = 2, over the 36-state grid {0.4, 0.5, 0.65, 0.8, 1.0, 1.15}^2 of (pair, other arm): T 0, FOUR 0, STUB 0, HT 0, TT 1 folded triangle (pair 0.8, other 0.4); every vertex on the union (residual 0.0000 R). p = 1: T 2, TT 1, HT 1; p = 3: HT 1, TT 1; 9 keys with p = 2: TT 1. The direct start on the same grid: T 2, FOUR 5, STUB 2, HT 3, TT 2. Not built in the shader: the keys' storage (per fillet vertex, per key, an offset set in the bones' frames: 12 floats, so a `keyTex` of 3 texels per key per vertex, read by the start) and the weights (the region pass would write each region's 4 weights next to its capsules). The oracle works in the rest pose with swell only (world positions); the real bake keeps the keys in the bone frames and must bake in both poses.

## A region's fillet dissolves in and out (plan owner, 22 Nov), `tests/net-switch.test.ts`
The arms' tubes run through their seams to the node (WP2), so a junction with no fillet is still whole. A region's fillet is built at the body layout (variant 0), at the map layout (variant 1) and, where those share no t, at the middle of the Circuit (variant 2); each is drawn by the screen dither (`SCREEN_DITHER_GLSL`, one string in materials/shaders.ts, the old program's text unchanged) with a share of its pixels: the body fillet dissolves over [a - 0.1, a], the map fillet comes in over [b, b + 0.05], a mid fillet does both, and between them the plain tubes show; `a` and `b` are the bake's (`NodeReport.switchT`, [b, a]; `switchT2` for a mid mesh). The share is `smoothstep(in) * (1 - smoothstep(out))` of t, the ramps in the region row (texel 3, `regionFades`), the CPU twin `regionShare`. Where the share is 0 the mesh is a point at the top of the vertex stage (two texel reads, no projection); the fragment stage discards the pixels whose pattern value is at or above the share, before any derivative. Region row: variant (texel 0.z), the ramps (texel 3), bones from `REGION_HEAD` (4); `vDraw` varying (10 of 15). Not done, WP2's and WP4's: the extended tubes, the bake table, the acceptance on the real regions at every 0.05 of t (no folded drawn triangle, no gap where a fillet dissolves, crotch ink drawn but not doubled), the per-frame wiring and the read budget. The 6.9 degree normal regression on H, STUB and HS is WP2's.

## The junction peel: tried and taken out (22 Nov)
A per-region peel share (every bone of a region at its node's share, the arms easing back over 3 radii) made the 32 real regions travel as one piece in the oracle but did not fix the unfolding, and the plan owner reverted it: the Circuit's 2D flow peel is exact again. `net/peel.ts` (`peelTable`) and the edge column `peelA` stay only because WP2's window search and WP4's rows still use them; the shader no longer reads them. Delete them with their last user.

## Also
Fades: the net shader evaluates each bone's free-end fade at the bone's own REST position (`position = A.xyz` in netBone), never at the pixel's y, so a hidden tail whose node sits in the fade band keeps its own alpha. Indices: `NetMesh.index` is a `Uint32Array`; the geometry must be uploaded as 32-bit (WebGL2), no split for size (WP4).


## Bake radius, from the acceptance on the real figure (22 Nov, after 81dbc18a)
Run on the real network (20 preset states of net-states.mjs, t = 0 to 1, my own oracle, not scripts/net-accept.mjs): five body-built fillets folded 1 to 10 triangles in nearly every state (CV_R, IVCS+IVC_IS, PVH_R_2, PVH_L_1, RHV_IVC_1; PVT_L and LHV_IVC at the high states), at t 0 to 0.2 where the body fillet is drawn. Root cause: ARM_RANGE has permanent arms with range [1, 1] (the hepatic and portal branches), and 'max12' baked them at 1 / 1.2, so they sat at 1.2 of their baked radius in EVERY state; the shader's start is the bone blend at the baked radii, and a uniform growth of more than about 10% folds it (uniform ratio 0.8 to 1.0: 0 to 2 triangles; 1.1: 0 to 1; 1.15: 0 to 8; 1.2: 1 to 12; 1.3: 2 to 29, over 9 of 17 regions). The bake now ('narrow', `tests/net-ranges.test.ts`): an arm whose range spans at most 1.2 is baked at the geometric mean of it (ratio about 1 in every state), a wider range with its top at 1.1 of the baked radius (it was 1.2). Result on network 1, five of the twenty states, t = 0, .1, .2: 0 folded triangles, 0 vertices off the union by more than 0.01 R, no gap. The wide arms' LOW side (0.4 of the baked radius at RHV_IVC_1 and LHV_IVC) is the keyed start's, still not in the shader.
